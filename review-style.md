# CEO Review Style

## Voice & Tone
- Direct and authoritative — no hedging language ("might", "could potentially", "it seems")
- Confident assertions, not suggestions
- Executive audience — assumes business literacy, not technical literacy
- Lead with human/patient impact before financial impact when both are present

## Structural Preferences
- Lead with business value, not technology
- Problem → Solution → Outcome structure
- Short paragraphs — maximum 3 sentences
- Active voice throughout

## Appian Domain Rules
- Always tie claims to specific Appian capabilities (process automation, case management, data fabric, AI skills)
- Avoid generic "digital transformation" language without grounding in Appian outcomes
- Reference customer ROI metrics when possible (time saved, cost reduced, cycle time)
- When naming AI capabilities, use "Agentic AI" — not "AI agents" as the lead concept or product label

## Phrases to Avoid
- "leverage" → use "use" or "apply"
- "utilize" → use "use"
- "in order to" → use "to"
- "synergy", "paradigm shift", "holistic approach"
- Passive constructions like "it was found that", "results were seen"
- "eliminate the bottleneck" → reframe around what the technology delivers, not what it removes
- Vague intensifiers like "exactly" in CTAs ("See exactly how…" → "See how…")
- Financial/valuation framing like "NPV" → use plain revenue language ("lost revenue", "revenue at risk") when describing business cost of delay
- Adverbs that overstate AI autonomy ("autonomously") → let the described actions carry the meaning

## Phrases CEO Prefers
- "accelerate" (for speed outcomes)
- "streamline" (for process efficiency)
- "measurable results"
- Specific numbers over vague claims
- "Agentic AI" (preferred term for AI agent capabilities in Appian context)
- Outcome framing that pairs patient/human impact with business impact (e.g., "patients wait — and revenue is lost")
- "lost revenue" over financial modeling terms like "NPV" when describing cost of delay to an executive audience

## AI Agent Description Patterns
- Describe AI agent actions in terms of specific, concrete tasks — not abstract categories
  - Prefer: "document generation, gap identification, proactive follow-ups"
  - Avoid: "document validation, version reconciliation, compliance checks" (too generic/process-internal)
- Contrast AI-handled coordination work against high-value human judgment work
  - Prefer: "so teams focus on scientific judgment, not coordination overhead"
  - Avoid: "so teams focus on review quality, not administrative coordination" (too vague)
- Name AI actions as noun phrases in a parallel list, not as verb phrases with adverbs
  - Prefer: "gap identification, proactive approval follow-ups"
  - Avoid: "flags compliance gaps, drives proactive follow-ups autonomously" (verb-led phrasing with redundant adverbs)

## Call-to-Action Style
- Clear, single CTA per piece
- Action-oriented: "See how [Company] did it" not "Learn more"
- CTAs should end on curiosity or next step — not on the product claim
  - Prefer: "…and what it takes to get there"
  - Avoid: "…and what that means for your R&D pipeline" (over-explains the value; let the reader arrive there)
