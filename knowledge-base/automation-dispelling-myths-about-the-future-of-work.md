# Automation: Dispelling myths about the Future of Work

*By Pramod Sachdeva*

The widespread belief that automation will completely replace human workers is unfounded. Instead, the future involves human and digital workers collaborating effectively as integrated teams.

## The Reality of Automation's Impact

Contrary to common myths, automation won't eliminate every role. While physical and basic cognitive skills may see a 15% reduction by 2030, demand for higher cognitive, social/emotional, and technical skills will surge by approximately 55% in the same period. Organizations recognize this shift and are strategically planning their automation initiatives accordingly.

## Re-imagining the Combined Workforce

Success requires thinking beyond workforce replacement to reimagine work itself. Digital workers excel at high-volume, repetitive tasks—invoice processing, data entry, file maintenance, and reconciliation. These "sweet spot" activities benefit from automation's speed, accuracy, and round-the-clock availability.

Meanwhile, humans should focus on work requiring judgment, deep knowledge, and experience. This includes customer interaction, creative thinking, and complex problem-solving. In product planning scenarios, robots analyze countless supply chain scenarios while humans apply experience and market insight to make final decisions.

## Mentoring a Robot

The emerging "new-collar jobs" require teaching digital workers to handle exceptions. A human mentor trains robots on specialized tasks while remaining available when the automation encounters unfamiliar situations—strengthening both components of the workforce.

## Conclusion

The future workforce succeeds through true collaboration between human and digital workers. Workforce automation is no longer optional but essential for meeting consumer needs and retaining talent. Implemented correctly, this partnership delivers superior customer experiences, enhanced productivity, and greater employee satisfaction.
