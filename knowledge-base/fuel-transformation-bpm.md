# Fuel Your Transformation with Business Process Management (BPM)

*By Pramod Sachdeva*

## BPM: Important as Ever

Organizations pursuing excellence must eventually transform their business processes. Business Process Management (BPM) serves as both a discipline and technology platform enabling continuous improvement cycles. While BPM is a mature model, its relevance has expanded significantly with newer technologies like Low-code, RPA, and AI.

For BPM to become a competitive advantage, companies must treat it as an ongoing capability rather than a one-time project. The BPM cycle itself—which is continuous by nature—"creates a flywheel of continuous improvements, better efficiency, and ultimately better company performance."

## BPM Phase 1 – Modeling: Where do You Start?

The foundation of process transformation is understanding existing processes. During the modeling phase, teams define processes comprehensively using cloud-based collaborative tools. This captures happy paths, exceptions, steps, systems, decisions, data, documents, and roles.

Simulations run against the model establish baselines and metrics like cycle times and resource requirements. Organizations can then test alternate scenarios. The modeling exercise itself often reveals improvement opportunities without requiring implementation. For example, a loan application simulation might identify underwriting as a bottleneck, prompting scenario testing to evaluate solutions like resource allocation or process redesign.

## BPM Phase 2 – Execution: Putting in the New Engine

The execution phase implements technical changes through a BPM engine. Data models are created, user interfaces are built, and system integrations are developed. This centralizes process execution rather than relying on disparate systems, emails, and spreadsheets.

Modern technologies—RPA, Low-code platforms, and AI—extend automation significantly. Routine manual tasks like data entry and file management become automated. The loan application example shifts to a centralized portal where customers enter personal information directly, improving speed and accuracy.

The BPM System provides additional benefits: improved metrics (faster cycle times, higher throughput) and automatic audit trails—especially valuable in regulated industries.

## BPM Phase 3 – Monitoring: Your Automated Process Supervisor

Monitoring capabilities provide visibility into process instances and identify problems. Issues once buried in communications become "readily visible often with the corrective action presented." This transforms reactive processes into proactive ones.

In the underwriting example, monitoring reveals incomplete applications causing delays. Process improvements can then address data gaps through earlier validation or direct customer data collection.

## BPM Phase 4 – Improvement: Continuing the Cycle

Heat map analytics aggregate process data over time to identify trends. Six months of loan application data might show that 70% of effort concentrates in underwriting and approval—providing hard evidence for improvement initiatives.

With processes already modeled and simulation capabilities available, teams can quickly test scenarios and implement cost-effective improvements. This establishes continuous improvement as an organizational capability.

## Summary: BPM Forever

Persistent organizational pressures—efficiency requirements, cost reduction, improved metrics—make BPM critical for sustainable digital transformation. "Every company is becoming a data company," and transformed processes naturally improve data quality throughout the organization.

Ultimately, BPM enables better customer experiences. In competitive markets, convenience and simplicity matter significantly, making process transformation a lasting business imperative.
