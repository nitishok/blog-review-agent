# Title Formulas

## Recurring Title Patterns

### Pattern 1: "How [Subject] [Verb Phrase]" — most common
Appears in ~10 of 30 titles. Signals a practical, how-to framing even when the post is more analytical than instructional.

Examples:
- "How AI-Powered Process Automation is Compressing Regulatory Timelines in Drug Development"
- "How Modern Teams Ace Regulatory Submissions: From Fragmentation to Flow"
- "How Process Automation and AI Minimize Human Error in the Drug Development Lifecycle"
- "How are leading pharma companies modernizing their labs?" (Shortcast 8)
- "How is RPA different from Workflow Automation?" (Shortcast 5)
- "How does Process Automation Streamline Regulatory Submissions?" (Shortcast 3)

### Pattern 2: "[Verb]ing [Noun] with [Technology/Approach]" — frequent
Action-oriented, positions the solution (technology) as the instrument.

Examples:
- "Solving Clinical Supply Chain Challenges with Process Automation"
- "Streamlining Lab Processes with LIMS Software Integration"
- "Streamlining Access to Clinical Study Data with Low-code Process Automation"
- "Solving Challenges in Clinical Supply Chain Management with Low-code Process Automation"
- "Navigating EU-CTR Compliance with Workflow Automation"
- "Transforming Global Drug Development Processes with Workflow Automation"

### Pattern 3: "[Adjective/Superlative Noun]: [Subtitle Explaining the Angle]" — for thought leadership
Used for the more ambitious, editorial posts. The colon splits a catchy label from a specific claim.

Examples:
- "Why Faster Clinical Trials Need More Than Automation: The Role of AI in Clinical Execution"
- "Agentic AI in Study Start-Up: Automating Documents, Approvals, and Site Activation"
- "Bridging the Legacy Gap: How Data Fabric Powers Life Sciences Integration"
- "How Modern Teams Ace Regulatory Submissions: From Fragmentation to Flow"
- "Smarter Site Selection in Clinical Trials: where Process Automation meets AI"
- "Accelerating Regulatory Submissions — How AI Agents are Eliminating the Last Mile of Manual Work in Pharma R&D"

### Pattern 4: Question Titles — used for educational and shortcast posts
Questions signal a definitional or myth-busting post. Dominant in the Shortcast series.

Examples:
- "Is Process Automation the Future of the Pharma Regulatory Labeling Process?"
- "Five Symptoms That Indicate You Need a Low-code Platform"
- "When Should Pharma Companies Start Looking at Workflow Automation?" (Shortcast 2)
- "What is Low-code Process Automation?" (Shortcast 6)

### Pattern 5: "The [Noun] of [Noun]" — occasional business case framing
- "The True Cost of Delays in Clinical and Regulatory Operations – and How to Reduce It"
- "Fuel Your Transformation with Business Process Management (BPM)"

## Title Length

Titles tend to be long — most are 10-18 words. Princeton Blue does not use short, punchy titles. This is consistent with B2B SEO priorities (keyword density) but reduces click-through appeal for social sharing.

Shortest titles: "Get High on Low-code" (5 words), "BPM and Low-code – Synergies for Success" (7 words)
Longest: "Accelerating Regulatory Submissions — How AI Agents are Eliminating the Last Mile of Manual Work in Pharma R&D" (18 words)

---

## Opportunities for Title Improvement

- Add urgency signals: "Why [X] Can No Longer Wait," "The Hidden Cost of [X]"
- Use numbers more: "5 Ways...," "3 Signs...," "The 1 Change That..."
- Shorten for social sharing: titles over 12 words lose impact in LinkedIn previews
- Use more active verbs: "eliminate," "accelerate," "cut," "unlock" instead of "streamline," "solving," "transforming"
